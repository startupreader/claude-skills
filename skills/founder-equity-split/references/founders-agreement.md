# Founders' agreement: terms to agree

A list of points for the founders to settle between themselves and then hand to a lawyer to draft. It is not a legal draft, and the answer must not present it as one.

## The terms

1. **The split.** Each founder's percentage and share count, and the scores behind it, so the reasoning survives.
2. **Vesting.** Length (commonly four years), cliff (commonly one year), monthly after the cliff, and any credit for time already worked, with the date vesting is counted from.
3. **Roles and decision-making.** Who does what, and how decisions are made. With two equal owners, say how a deadlock is settled.
4. **Leaving.** What happens to unvested shares (forfeited, or bought back by the company, often at a nominal price), the price for vested shares if the company or the other founders can buy them, and good and bad leaver terms.
5. **Acceleration.** Whether unvested shares vest early if the company is sold: single trigger (on the sale) or double trigger (on the sale, and only if the founder then loses their role).
6. **Money put in.** Treated as a loan or as an investment on the same terms as outside investors, written down, so the split stays a reward for work.
7. **Intellectual property.** Code, designs and anything else built for the company, before and after incorporation, assigned to the company.
8. **Vesting at a funding round.** Investors may ask founders to restart vesting from the round. Agree now what credit for time already worked the founders will ask for.

## Good and bad leavers

Agreements often treat founders differently depending on why they leave. A good leaver (for example, leaving through illness, or let go without cause) may keep vested shares and sometimes get a fair price for them. A bad leaver (for example, leaving to join a competitor, or dismissed for misconduct) may have to sell even vested shares back at a low price. These clauses are negotiated: list which events the founders want in each group, and flag that the definitions need a lawyer.

## Where it is written

- Before outside investment: a founders' agreement, with founders' shares issued and vesting and the IP assigned. Pre-seed investors look for exactly that.
- Founders already own their shares, so founder vesting is set up as reverse vesting: the company can take back or buy back the unvested part if a founder leaves.
- Once the company raises, founder vesting is usually written into the shareholders' agreement, so the founders should settle it between themselves first.

## Mistakes to call out

- Splitting by who had the idea. In the weighted model it moves the split by about two points.
- No vesting, so a co-founder who leaves early keeps a large stake (often called dead equity).
- Paying for cash with equity instead of treating it as a loan or an investment.
- Equity promised in chats and emails without recording it; such promises tend to add up to more than planned.
- Looking only at the split at founding. Model each founder's stake after the option pool and a few rounds.
