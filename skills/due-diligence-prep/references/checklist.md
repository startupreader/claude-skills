Rules in these areas change with budgets, RBI Master Directions and MCA notifications: tell the founders to confirm current requirements on mca.gov.in and rbi.org.in, or with an adviser.

## How diligence runs

After the term sheet is signed, the investor's lawyers (and sometimes accountants) send a request list. The company shares documents in a data room and answers follow-up questions, and the advisers write a report. Each finding is then:
- fixed before closing, as a condition precedent;
- disclosed and accepted; or
- covered by indemnities in the transaction documents.

Angel rounds usually involve a lighter review of the basics. There is no fixed timeline: as a rough guide from practitioners, a seed round often takes two to four weeks of diligence and a Series A four to six, and gaps found along the way add time. Closing after a term sheet commonly takes one to three months in all.

## 1. Corporate records
- Certificate of incorporation.
- Memorandum and articles of association, and every amendment.
- Board minutes and resolutions.
- Shareholder minutes and resolutions.
- **(India)** Statutory registers, including the register of members.
- Founders' agreement and any earlier investment agreements (in India, usually a share subscription agreement and a shareholders' agreement for each priced round).
- **(India)** Annual filings with the Registrar of Companies: financial statements and the annual return.
- **(India)** Demat status. Most private companies other than small companies must now issue and hold securities in dematerialised form, with a compliance deadline of 30 June 2025, so investors check it.

## 2. Cap table and ESOP
- A cap table on a fully diluted basis: issued shares, all options (granted and unallocated pool) and convertible instruments (SAFEs, notes, CCDs, CCPS) as if converted.
- The cap table must reconcile with the legal record: in India, the register of members, share certificates or demat records, and every return of allotment (Form PAS-3) filed with the Registrar. Investors' lawyers reconcile these at every round.
- For each past share issue: the valuation report, the approvals, the offer letter and the filings.
- **(India)** Valuation reports. Shares issued to a non-resident cannot be priced below fair value certified under FEMA; a preferential allotment by an unlisted company needs a registered valuer's report under company law. Keep a report for every round, dated close to the allotment.
- The ESOP plan, with its approval. **(India)** The plan must be approved by shareholders by special resolution.
- Option grant letters and vesting records.
- A US parent company that grants stock options (for example after a flip) needs a 409A valuation before the first grant, at least every 12 months while it keeps granting, and after a material event such as a new round.

## 3. Finance
- Audited financial statements.
- Recent management accounts.
- Bank statements.

## 4. Tax
- **(India)** GST registrations and returns, with reconciliations.
- **(India)** TDS deducted and deposited on salaries, contractor payments and rent, and the related returns.
- Income tax returns, assessments and any notices. **(India)** Including old angel tax cases: the provision stopped applying from 1 April 2025, but assessments and appeals for earlier years continue under the old rule.
- **(India)** Provident fund and ESI registrations and payments, where they apply.

## 5. Intellectual property
- IP assignments from founders, employees and contractors to the company. Check everyone who built product, code, design or content, including before incorporation.
- Trademarks and domains held in the company's name, not a founder's.

## 6. Contracts
- Key customer, supplier and partner contracts, especially their change-of-control, exclusivity and liability clauses.

## 7. People
- Employment contracts with confidentiality and invention assignment terms.
- **(India)** An internal committee under the POSH Act if the company has 10 or more employees.
- How personal data is handled. **(India)** The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 applies; its rules were notified in November 2025 and are being phased in.

## 8. FEMA (Indian companies with foreign investors)
Investors look closely at these, and lapses take the longest to fix.

| Event | Filing or action | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Money received from a foreign investor | Issue the shares, or refund | Issue within 60 days; refund within 15 days after that |
| Shares issued to a foreign investor | Form FC-GPR on the RBI's FIRMS portal | Within 30 days of issue |
| Convertible notes issued | Form CN | Within 30 days of issue |
| Share transfer between a resident and a non-resident | Form FC-TRS | 60 days from transfer or payment, whichever is earlier |
| Company holding foreign investment | Annual FLA return | By 15 July each year |
| Downstream investment by a foreign-owned company | Form DI | Within 30 days of allotment |

Also check:
- A fair value certificate behind each foreign issue, from a chartered accountant, a SEBI-registered merchant banker or a practising cost accountant. The issue price must be at least that fair value. For convertible instruments, the price or conversion formula must be fixed at issue.
- Instruments: foreign investors can hold equity shares, fully and compulsorily convertible preference shares and debentures (CCPS, CCDs), share warrants, and convertible notes from DPIIT-recognised startups only. A US-style SAFE is not one of the listed instruments.
- Beneficial ownership of each foreign investor against the rules on investment from countries sharing a land border with India (government approval since Press Note 3 of 2020; a March 2026 clarification lets non-controlling beneficial ownership of up to 10% from such countries use the automatic route, subject to conditions). Check the current wording before relying on it.
- The sector's entry route and any cap.

Late filings attract a late submission fee set by the RBI; bigger lapses may need compounding with the RBI, which takes time and can delay a round.

## 9. Regulatory, litigation and disclosures
- Sector licences the business needs.
- Pending litigation and regulatory notices.
- A short disclosure list of known issues. Investors trust problems the founders raise first.

## Red flags and how each is usually resolved

| Red flag | What to check | Usual fix | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cap table does not match the register of members, share certificates or demat records, or the PAS-3 filings | Reconcile holder by holder, issue by issue | Correct the cap table or the records, with the company's advisers | Depends on the gap |
| IP still owned by a founder or contractor | Signed assignment from every founder and every contractor who built product, code, design or content | Get assignments signed now by founders and contractors | Quick if people are reachable |
| Unsigned or draft agreements in the data room | Final signed copy exists | Replace with the signed copy, or get it signed | Quick |
| Missing board or shareholder approval for a share issue or the ESOP plan | Minutes and resolutions for every issue and the plan | Ratify the approval | Quick to medium |
| ESOP plan without shareholder approval (India: special resolution) | Plan date against the resolution date | Pass the resolution; review grants made before it | Medium |
| Unfiled or late ROC forms (PAS-3, annual filings) | Filing acknowledgements | Late company filings can usually be made with additional fees | Medium |
| Missing or late FEMA filings (FC-GPR, FC-TRS, FLA) | Acknowledgements, dates against the deadlines above | Late submission fee; compounding with the RBI for bigger lapses | Long: start early |
| Share issue to a foreign investor without a fair value certificate | Certificate dated close to the issue, price at or above fair value | Take advice: may need regularising | Long |
| Undisclosed disputes, tax notices or old angel tax cases | Notices, correspondence, assessments | Put on the disclosure list | Quick to disclose |
| No POSH internal committee with 10 or more employees (India) | Constitution of the committee | Constitute it | Quick to medium |

"Quick", "medium" and "long" are relative: the explainers give no fixed durations, so do not promise any.

## Setting up the data room
- Run the company's own review first and fix gaps: file missed forms, ratify approvals, get IP assignments signed.
- One folder per area (corporate, cap table and ESOPs, finance, tax, IP, contracts, people, FEMA, litigation), with an index in each.
- Name files clearly and keep only final, signed versions.
- Give named access rather than open links, and keep a log of questions and answers.
- Prepare a short disclosure list of known issues.

## Companies outside India
Sections 1 to 3 and 5 to 7 broadly apply everywhere, as do the red flags on IP, approvals, the cap table and unsigned documents. The India items (ROC forms, statutory registers, demat, GST, TDS, PF and ESI, POSH, FEMA) have local equivalents that this checklist does not cover: ask the founders' lawyer or company secretary for the local list rather than guessing form names or deadlines.
